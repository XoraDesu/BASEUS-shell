# BASEUS — Anti-Slop Quality System

Anti-slop does not mean generic minimalism. It means intentional design.

## The Five-Question Test

For every new element, ask:

1. What does it do?
2. Why is it here?
3. Why this size/prominence?
4. Why this behavior/motion?
5. Why does it feel BASEUS?

Then ask what becomes worse if it is removed.

## Patterns to Reject

### Cardification
Do not turn every piece of information into a card. Containment must improve comprehension or interaction.

### Container Addiction
Avoid unnecessary backgrounds, borders, shadows, padding boxes, and decorative surfaces.

### Rounded-Everything
Radius is contextual, not mandatory.

### Gradient Abuse
Gradients are exceptions. Solid surfaces are the default.

### Glassmorphism
Do not use blur/glass merely to look premium.

### Neon / RGB / Cyberpunk Abuse
Cyberpunk belongs primarily to motion and effects, not the permanent visual language.

### Decorative UI
No fake metrics, random lines, blobs, particles, badges, or meters used solely to fill space.

### Dashboard Syndrome
Home and Headquarter are not generic analytics dashboards.

### Screenshot-First Design
Optimize interaction, hierarchy, discoverability, and performance rather than screenshots.

### Animation Slop
Animation needs a communicative reason. Avoid arbitrary bounce, excessive easing, or constant motion.

### Microinteraction Overload
Not every hover, click, scroll, and state needs a special animation.

### Icon Slop
Icons must communicate meaning. Typography can be better.

### Emoji Slop
Emoji is not a UI design system.

### Typography Slop
Avoid giant hero text, random uppercase labels, and fake editorial decoration.

### Asymmetry Slop
Intentional asymmetry is not random misalignment.

### Empty-Space Abuse
Wabi-sabi is not an excuse to make everything sparse without hierarchy.

### Personalization Slop
Do not expose hundreds of meaningless pixel-level options. Personalization should matter.

### Settings Slop
Use the user's mental model. Avoid developer terminology and endless advanced controls.

### Feature Slop
Do not add features merely because another desktop environment has them.

### Complexity Creep
Simple means complexity is hidden until needed, not that the system is shallow.

### Generic “Modern UI” Slop
Translate vague adjectives such as “premium” or “futuristic” into concrete BASEUS rules before implementing them.

### Copywriting Slop
Avoid meaningless filler copy.

### Empty-State Slop
An empty state may simply be empty.

### Notification Slop
Do not duplicate persistent information as notifications or create giant banners for trivial events.

### Performance Slop
Unnecessary polling, loops, recomputation, background services, and hidden surfaces are quality failures too.

### Accessibility Slop
Minimalism cannot justify inaccessible interaction.

### Consistency vs Uniformity
Shared principles are required; identical shapes, spacing, radii, and sizes everywhere are not.

## Removal Test

If removing an element does not meaningfully hurt comprehension, function, hierarchy, or identity, question whether it belongs.

## BASEUS Identity Test

Could this element be moved into another Linux shell and remain visually indistinguishable? If yes, review it.

## Final Review Gate

Before accepting a UI change, verify:

- purpose is clear
- MASTER/DESIGN are respected
- no accepted decision is contradicted
- hierarchy and whitespace are intentional
- containers/icons are justified
- personalization is meaningful
- complexity is hidden until needed
- usability works beyond screenshots
- performance remains acceptable
- accessibility remains intact
- the result is recognizably BASEUS
